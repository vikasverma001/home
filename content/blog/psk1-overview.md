The **Professional Scrum with Kanban I (PSK-I)** is one of the more nuanced Scrum.org assessments because it tests two overlapping bodies of knowledge: the **Scrum Guide (2020)** and the **Kanban Guide for Scrum Teams**. Getting both right — and knowing where they interact — is what separates a pass from a near-miss.

This series is a topic-by-topic breakdown of everything the exam covers, built for people who already have some Scrum experience and want to go deep rather than wide.

## The exam at a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Questions** | 45 |
| **Time** | 60 minutes |
| **Passing score** | 85% (≈ 39 correct; you can miss ~6) |
| **Question types** | Multiple-choice, multiple-answer, true/false |
| **Format** | Short scenarios + direct knowledge checks |
| **Free practice** | Scrum with Kanban Open on scrum.org |

## What's actually testable

Everything comes from two sources:

1. **The Scrum Guide (2020)** — roles, events, artifacts, rules
2. **The Kanban Guide for Scrum Teams** — the four Kanban practices, flow metrics, Definition of Workflow, SLE

A few deeper topics (Monte Carlo simulation, Little's Law assumptions, percentile mechanics) come from PSK courseware and flow literature that *builds on* the Kanban Guide. When in doubt, the guide wording is what's graded.

## The one principle that prevents most wrong answers

> **Scrum is still Scrum.**

Kanban adds four *practices* that improve *flow*. It never adds roles, events, or artifacts. It never changes Scrum's structure. Any answer option implying Kanban alters how Scrum is organised is almost always wrong.

## How to use this series

- **First read**: go in order (A → H). Each part builds on the one before.
- **Drilling a weakness**: jump directly to the relevant part and work through the "how questions come" blocks.
- **Before the exam**: work through Part H last — it's the meta-layer that makes the rest click faster under time pressure.
- **Benchmark**: aim for 95%+ on the **Scrum with Kanban Open** repeatedly before booking the real assessment.
