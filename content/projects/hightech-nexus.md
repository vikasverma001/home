<section class="cs-section"><h2>The challenge</h2><p>Five Scrum teams worked on one product but planned in isolation. Integration happened late and painfully, dependencies surfaced mid-sprint, and "done" meant something different on every team.</p><p>The org wanted the benefits of multiple teams without the chaos — a single, integrated, releasable increment every sprint.</p></section>
<section class="cs-section"><h2>Approach</h2><div class="cs-phases"><div class="cs-phase"><div class="cs-phase-num">01</div><div class="cs-phase-body"><h3>Assessment</h3><p>Mapped team topology, dependencies, and the real sources of integration pain before changing any process.</p></div></div><div class="cs-phase"><div class="cs-phase-num">02</div><div class="cs-phase-body"><h3>Nexus setup</h3><p>Introduced the Nexus framework — a single product backlog, a Nexus Integration Team, and shared events.</p></div></div><div class="cs-phase"><div class="cs-phase-num">03</div><div class="cs-phase-body"><h3>Unified definition of done</h3><p>Negotiated one definition of done across all five teams so increments were genuinely integratable.</p></div></div><div class="cs-phase"><div class="cs-phase-num">04</div><div class="cs-phase-body"><h3>Cross-team planning</h3><p>Ran Nexus Sprint Planning to surface dependencies up front and sequence work to minimize integration risk.</p></div></div><div class="cs-phase"><div class="cs-phase-num">05</div><div class="cs-phase-body"><h3>Coaching &amp; cadence</h3><p>Coached Scrum Masters and POs through the first cycles until the rhythm held without intervention.</p></div></div></div></section>
<section class="cs-section"><h2>How it fit together</h2><div class="cs-arch"><div class="cs-arch-row"><div class="cs-arch-tag">Single backlog</div><p>One ordered product backlog feeds all five teams, owned by a single Product Owner.</p></div><div class="cs-arch-row"><div class="cs-arch-tag">Integration team</div><p>A Nexus Integration Team owns integration concerns and a shared definition of done.</p></div><div class="cs-arch-row"><div class="cs-arch-tag">Shared events</div><p>Nexus-level planning, review, and retrospective wrap the individual team events.</p></div><div class="cs-arch-row"><div class="cs-arch-tag">Dependency view</div><p>Dependencies are made visible and sequenced during cross-team planning.</p></div></div><div class="cs-diagram">Architecture / flow diagram — to add</div></section>
<section class="cs-section"><h2>Outcomes</h2><ul class="cs-outcomes"><li>Five teams now produce one integrated, releasable increment each sprint.</li><li>Integration moved from a late, risky event to a continuous, managed activity.</li><li>Dependencies are surfaced during planning instead of exploding mid-sprint.</li><li>A shared definition of done raised quality consistency across all teams.</li></ul></section>

<section class="cs-section cs-reflection"><h2>Reflections</h2><p>Nexus is deliberately minimal, and that is its strength. The hard part was social, not structural — getting five teams to commit to one definition of done and one backlog order.</p></section>
